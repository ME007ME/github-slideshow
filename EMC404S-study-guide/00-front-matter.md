# How to use this study guide

This study guide covers every learning outcome from **Outcome 39 to Outcome 65** of the EMC404S study guide, written out under the exact outcome wording, in full sentences, with in-text citations and a single consolidated reference list at the end of the document. Each outcome is written from the point of view of how an Emergency Care Practitioner (ECP) in South Africa would actually assess and manage the patient in the prehospital environment, within the South African legal framework (the Constitution, the Mental Health Care Act 17 of 2002, the National Health Act 61 of 2003, the Children's Act 38 of 2005, the Criminal Law (Sexual Offences and Related Matters) Amendment Act 32 of 2007, the Domestic Violence Act 116 of 1998 as amended, and the HPCSA ethical rules and guidelines).

The documents that you uploaded have been used as primary sources throughout and are cited by author and year, namely Evans, Geduld and Stassen (2018); Tilley et al. (2026); the HPCSA Booklet 7 on withholding and withdrawing treatment (HPCSA, 2023); Saucedo Uribe et al. (2025); Desai, Kulkarni and Verma (n.d.); the Royal College of Emergency Medicine guideline on Acute Behavioural Disturbance (RCEM, 2025); Mesthrie (2025); Hüser et al. (2025); Kaswa (2024); and the three EMC404S tutorial memoranda. Additional trusted sources (legislation, DSM-5-TR, peer-reviewed reviews in journals such as *The Lancet* and *The New England Journal of Medicine*, WHO guidance, and standard emergency medicine and toxicology texts) have been added where your documents do not cover an outcome.

## Important note on drug doses and scope of practice

Your scope of practice as an ECP in South Africa is defined by the HPCSA Professional Board for Emergency Care (PBEC). The PBEC's Clinical Practice Guidelines (CPGs) were published in 2018, and the PBEC has since released the **Clinical Decision Support Tools for Emergency Care Providers (CDST), South Africa 2024, version 1.3, October 2025** (HPCSA PBEC, 2025). The CDST was commissioned and funded by the PBEC, developed by the Knowledge Translation Unit of the University of Cape Town, and is based on the 2018 CPGs together with the latest synthesised evidence. In the CDST, black text applies to all registered categories (BAA, AEA, ECA, ECT, ANT, ECP); \*green text with stars\* applies to AEA, ECA, ECT, ANT and ECP (not all green medicines are within AEA/ECA scope); ^orange text with carets^ applies only to ANT and ECP; and ~purple text with curvy lines~ applies only to ECPs (HPCSA PBEC, 2025, p. 3).

**In this guide, every prehospital drug and dose for South African practice is taken from the CDST v1.3 (HPCSA PBEC, 2025) wherever the CDST addresses the condition, and is labelled "CDST".** Where the CDST does not address a condition (for example lithium, iron or paracetamol overdose, envenomation or sexual assault PEP), doses come from the other documents you supplied (for example RCEM, 2025; Hüser et al., 2025) or from peer-reviewed and South African national guidance, and are labelled as **hospital-level or reference information** so that you know they are not prehospital ECP scope. If your service's medication protocol differs from the CDST, follow your employer's protocol in practice. In the examination, answer according to the CDST and what your lecturers have taught.

## Corrections you should know about

1. Outcome 50.1 in the study guide refers to the "Metal Health Care Act (Act 15 of 2004)". The correct citation is the **Mental Health Care Act 17 of 2002**, which came into operation on 15 December 2004 (Mental Health Care Act 17 of 2002; Evans, Geduld and Stassen, 2018). The Act was subsequently amended by the Mental Health Care Amendment Act 12 of 2014; the section numbers and wording quoted in this guide (ss 9, 32, 33, 34 and 40) are those of the current consolidated Act.
2. The Toxicology Tutorial 1 memo describes sorbitol-containing charcoal as "emetic". Sorbitol is an osmotic **cathartic** (laxative), not an emetic; it is avoided because it causes diarrhoea, fluid loss and electrolyte disturbance, particularly in infants, and because cathartics provide no proven benefit (Hüser et al., 2025). Kaswa (2024) also uses the word "emetic", so note that your lecturers may accept it, but the pharmacologically correct term is cathartic.
3. The Behavioural Emergencies Tutorial 1 memo refers to the "Mental Capacity Act" in the context of restraint. That is the **United Kingdom** Mental Capacity Act 2005 that the RCEM (2025) guideline refers to. **It does not apply in South Africa.** In South Africa the equivalent legal authority for treating a patient without consent comes from section 9(1)(c) of the Mental Health Care Act, section 7(1)(e) of the National Health Act, the common-law defence of necessity, and the HPCSA ethical guidelines on consent.

# Five-day study plan

You have five days. The plan below divides the material so that every outcome is covered once in detail and the highest-yield material is revisited on the final day. Each day assumes roughly six to eight hours of focused study, broken into 50-minute blocks with 10-minute breaks.

| Day | Material | Active-recall task at the end of the day |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Outcomes 39–44: definition and causes of behavioural emergencies, psychiatric signs and symptoms, interviewing, MSE and MMSE, principles of care, restraint ethics, violent patients | Without notes, write out the MSE headings, the ten domains of verbal de-escalation, and the organic "red flags" that suggest a medical cause of behaviour. Draw a flow diagram of your scene approach to a violent patient. |
| **Day 2** | Outcomes 45–51: suicide risk assessment and interview questions, legal duties in self-harm, abscondment, children and adolescents, older persons, the Mental Health Care Act, consent on behalf of incompetent patients, rights violations | Write out sections 9, 32, 33, 34 and 40 of the Mental Health Care Act in your own words. Write ten suicide-risk interview questions from memory. Answer the Behavioural Emergencies Tutorial 1 and 2 questions (Appendix A) without looking. |
| **Day 3** | Outcome 52 (DSM-5-TR disorders) and Outcomes 53–57 (sexual assault and domestic violence) | Make a one-page table of each DSM category: key features, emergency risks, prehospital management. Write out the elements of rape and sexual assault (sections 3 and 5 of the Sexual Offences Act), the 72-hour PEP rule, evidence preservation steps, and the protective measures of the Domestic Violence Act. |
| **Day 4** | Outcomes 58, 59, 61, 62 (routes, epidemiology, emesis, general management, toxidromes, activated charcoal, unknown/mixed overdose) and Outcomes 60.1–60.16 | Draw the toxidrome table from memory. List the contraindications to activated charcoal. Write the antidote for each poison in 60.1–60.16. |
| **Day 5** | Outcomes 60.17–60.32 and Outcomes 63–65 (plants, mushrooms, traditional medicine, envenomation, scombroid). Final revision of Appendix A (tutorial questions) and the "High-yield points" boxes throughout | Answer every Toxicology Tutorial 1 question (Appendix A) under timed conditions. Re-read every "High-yield points" box in the guide. |

**Study technique.** After reading each outcome, close the guide and write down or say aloud everything you remember (the "blurting" or free-recall method); this active recall produces much better retention than re-reading. Revisit material at increasing intervals (spaced repetition) by re-testing Day 1 material briefly on Day 3 and Day 5.
